# Buyer Economics: Generate vs Build vs Buy Outcome

## Canonical value model

The customer is buying back time, attention and execution risk. This applies to experts, adjacent professionals, companies, and non-technical buyers.

The internal `[HYPOTHESIS]` comparison is:

```text
BUY OUTCOME = Outcome Price + Buyer Integration Effort + Residual Risk

DIY / BUILD = Tool Cost + Buyer Time Cost + Expected Rework
              + Opportunity Cost + Residual Risk
```

This equation guides research and product economics. It is not a public mathematical guarantee, a universal superiority claim, or a substitute for observed acceptance and total-delivery data.

## Three alternatives

### Generate

Self-production with generic AI can be fast and effective. Its possible DIY AI Tax is `[HYPOTHESIS]` and may include tool access, learning, specification, iteration, QA, rework, orchestration, and opportunity cost. No numeric tax is asserted without evidence.

### Build

For developers and companies, building internally may include integration, maintenance, security, QA, operational support, project delay, and foregone differentiated work. Build remains rational when control, frequency, differentiation, or risk requirements justify it.

### Buy Outcome

Buying becomes attractive when a bounded price and integration effort are lower than the buyer's full Generate/Build burden, and when evidence reduces enough residual risk. Product class determines the verifier profile; a trust claim is only as strong as its recorded checks.

## Audience-specific evaluation

- Developer: opportunity cost, non-differentiating engineering, integration effort, maintenance, and quality history.
- Company: total delivery cost, time-to-outcome, QA/security/integration, procurement, and support.
- Non-technical buyer: tool learning, prompt/specification skill, retries, QA, and multi-tool orchestration.
- Adjacent creative/automation professional: repetitive production, cross-skill execution, and technical QA.

## Future measurement

The following are planned definitions, not implemented analytics:

- `BuyerEffortToAcceptedOutcome`
- `EstimatedBuyerTimeSaved`
- `BuyReason`: `TIME | QUALITY | SKILL_GAP | URGENCY | PRICE | TRUST | CONVENIENCE | OTHER`
- `AlternativePath`: `GENERATE | BUILD | HIRE | DO_NOTHING`
- `RemainingIntegrationEffort`

`CrossCategoryRepeatRate` counts transitions between different versioned Outcome categories for the same buyer, inside a declared measurement window, using accepted purchases only. It records the transition matrix and category versions. Views, clicks, abandoned configurations, and unaccepted purchases do not count.

No business threshold is defined until real data exists.
